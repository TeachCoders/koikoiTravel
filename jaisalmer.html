Everything About Jaisalmer

Essential travel guide, top attractions, weather, local tips & culture for Jaisalmer.

Famous For

  • Golden City – Famous for its golden-yellow sandstone buildings that glow in the sunlight.

  • Jaisalmer Fort (Sonar Quila) – One of the world's few living forts and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

  • Sam Sand Dunes – Popular for desert safaris, camel rides, jeep safaris, and cultural evenings.

  • Patwon Ki Haveli – A beautifully carved historic haveli known for its intricate architecture.

  • Gadisar Lake – A scenic lake ideal for boating and photography.

  • Desert Camping – Luxury camps, folk music, Kalbelia dance, and traditional Rajasthani cuisine.

  • Kuldhara Village – A famous abandoned village associated with local legends.

  • Shopping – Camel leather products, embroidered textiles, mirror work, silver jewelry, and handicrafts.

  • Food – Dal Baati Churma, Ker Sangri, Ghotua Ladoo, Kachori, and traditional Rajasthani dishes.

  • Best Time to Visit – October to March, especially during the Jaisalmer Desert Fes.

Jaisalmer: The Golden City That Still Lives Inside Its Fort

Jaisalmer earns its nickname honestly — at sunrise and sunset, the entire city glows a warm, golden yellow, built almost entirely from the same sandstone quarried right out of the surrounding Thar Desert. But what makes Jaisalmer genuinely rare isn't just the colour, it's that its fort isn't a museum piece cordoned off from daily life — people still live, trade and pray inside its walls, making it one of the very few "living forts" left in the world.

Top Monuments in Jaisalmer

  • Jaisalmer Fort (Sonar Quila) — a UNESCO World Heritage Site and the city's centrepiece, a living fort with homes, shops and temples still functioning inside its walls

  • Patwon Ki Haveli — a cluster of five ornate havelis built by a wealthy merchant family, considered the grandest haveli complex in the city

  • Nathmal Ki Haveli — known for its intricately carved twin facades, built by two brothers who reportedly worked on either half without coordinating designs

  • Salim Singh Ki Haveli — recognisable by its distinctive peacock-shaped brackets and unusual upper-floor architecture

  • Gadisar Lake — a historic water reservoir surrounded by temples and shrines, popular for a quiet boat ride

  • Jain Temples inside Jaisalmer Fort — a cluster of intricately carved temples dating from the 12th to 15th centuries, among the fort's most detailed architectural highlights

Monument Timings and Entry Fees

Jaisalmer Fort: Generally accessible throughout the day, though individual sections like the Jain temples and palace museum inside have their own specific hours and modest entry fees.

Patwon Ki Haveli: Open daily, roughly 9:00 AM–5:00 PM. Entry fees are modest, typically in the range of ₹20-60 for Indian visitors, with photography and videography sometimes charged separately.

Nathmal Ki Haveli: No formal entry fee for the main facade and courtyard, making it an easy, unhurried add-on to a Jaisalmer Fort visit.

Salim Singh Ki Haveli: Charges a modest entry fee, and it's worth setting aside 30-45 minutes here — the upper floors offer good views over the city and out toward the desert edges.

Note: Entry fees for Jaisalmer's havelis and monuments are revised periodically, so it's worth confirming current rates before you visit.

Sam Sand Dunes and the Desert Experience

No trip to Jaisalmer feels complete without heading out to the Sam Sand Dunes, roughly 35-40 km from the city, where the classic image of Rajasthan's desert — rolling golden sand with barely any vegetation — comes fully to life. Camel safaris and jeep safaris are both popular ways to explore the dunes, and many visitors time their trip for sunset, when the shifting light turns the sand a deep amber colour. For a quieter, less crowded alternative, the Khuri Sand Dunes offer a similar experience with fewer tour buses. Desert camps around both areas typically combine the safari with an evening of folk music, dance and a bonfire dinner under the stars — touristy, sure, but genuinely worth doing at least once.

Best Food to Try in Jaisalmer

Jaisalmer's desert setting has shaped its food in interesting ways — Ker Sangri, made from wild desert beans and berries, is a genuinely unique regional dish you won't easily find outside western Rajasthan. Dal Baati Churma remains a staple here too, as does Bajre ki Roti, a millet flatbread often paired with garlic chutney and local ghee. Given the town's strong Jain merchant history, you'll also find excellent Jain vegetarian food across the old city, prepared without onion or garlic but still packed with flavour.

Best Shopping in Jaisalmer

Jaisalmer's markets, particularly around the fort and Sadar Bazaar, are known for mirror-work textiles, embroidered bags and cushion covers, along with leather Mojari shoes and antique-style silver jewellery. The city is also a good place to pick up hand-carved wooden items and stone handicrafts, given the region's long history of sandstone and woodworking craftsmanship.

Dress Code in Jaisalmer

Jaisalmer is fairly traditional, so modest clothing is appreciated, particularly at the Jain temples inside the fort, where covering shoulders and legs is expected. Desert heat during the day calls for light, breathable fabrics, but evenings — especially out at the sand dunes — can turn surprisingly cold, so layering matters more than it might seem given how hot the daytime can feel.

Culture of Jaisalmer

Jaisalmer's culture is deeply tied to its history as a trading post on the old caravan routes between India and Central Asia, which is part of why its havelis are so ornate — built by merchant families who grew wealthy on that trade and wanted their homes to show it. The fort's status as a living settlement gives Jaisalmer a rare, lived-in character most other Rajasthani forts have lost, with locals going about ordinary daily life against a backdrop most cities would rope off as a museum. Desert folk music and dance remain a genuine local tradition here too, not just a performance staged for tourists, still passed down through specific communities associated with the region.

Major Festivals in Jaisalmer

  • Desert Festival (Jan/Feb): Held against the backdrop of the Sam Sand Dunes, featuring folk music, camel races, turban-tying competitions and a genuinely festive desert atmosphere.

  • Gangaur (Mar/Apr): Celebrated with the same regional devotion as the rest of Rajasthan, marked by processions of decorated idols through the old city.

  • Diwali in Jaisalmer: The fort and havelis lit up with lamps during Diwali make for a particularly striking version of the festival, given the city's golden sandstone backdrop.

Language Spoken in Jaisalmer

  • Hindi is understood and spoken throughout the city and works almost everywhere.

  • Marwari, a Rajasthani dialect specific to the region, is the everyday language for most locals.

  • English is common among guides, camel safari operators and hotel staff, given how heavily Jaisalmer features on the international tourist circuit.

Traveller tip: Since people genuinely live inside Jaisalmer Fort, it's worth being mindful while wandering its lanes — many of the homes and shops are private residences, not just tourist backdrops, and a little respect goes a long way.

One Day Itinerary Suggestion

Morning exploring Jaisalmer Fort and its Jain temples, followed by Patwon Ki Haveli and Nathmal Ki Haveli in the late morning, an afternoon boat ride at Gadisar Lake, and an evening drive out to Sam Sand Dunes for sunset, camel rides and a desert camp dinner under the stars.

Best Time to Visit Jaisalmer

October to March is by far the best window, with cool, manageable desert temperatures ideal for both city sightseeing and dune safaris. Summers (April to June) turn brutally hot, often crossing 45°C, and are genuinely difficult for daytime exploration, so it's best avoided unless your trip has no other option.

How to Reach Jaisalmer

Jaisalmer has its own airport with limited but growing flight connectivity, mainly from Delhi and Jaipur. The city is well connected by rail too, with direct overnight trains from Delhi and Jaipur being a popular, comfortable way to arrive. Road trips are common as well, particularly as the final leg of a wider Rajasthan loop through Jodhpur, though the drive from Jodhpur to Jaisalmer takes a good several hours across fairly open desert highway.
