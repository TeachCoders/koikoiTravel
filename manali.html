Manali: The Adventure Capital of Himachal

Nestled in the Kullu Valley along the Beas River, Manali is one of Himachal Pradesh's most popular hill destinations, drawing everyone from honeymooners to serious adventure sports enthusiasts. Framed by snow-capped peaks, apple orchards, and dense pine forests, Manali also serves as the gateway to Ladakh via the Rohtang Pass and Leh-Manali Highway, giving it a dual identity as both a relaxed hill retreat and a launchpad for high-altitude adventure.

Things You Really Shouldn't Skip

Hadimba Devi Temple — A striking wooden temple set amid a cedar forest, dedicated to Hadimba, wife of Bhima from the Mahabharata, notable for its distinctive pagoda-style architecture built in 1553 — one of Manali's most atmospheric and photographed sites.

Solang Valley — A hub for adventure sports, offering paragliding, zorbing, and cable car rides in summer, and skiing in winter, all set against a dramatic backdrop of snow-covered peaks.

Rohtang Pass — A high-altitude mountain pass at over 3,900 metres, offering spectacular snow-covered scenery and access to the road toward Ladakh, though permits are required and the pass closes entirely in winter due to heavy snow.

Old Manali — A laidback, café-lined neighbourhood across the river from the main town, popular with backpackers for its relaxed vibe, live music cafes, and easy access to short walks along the Manalsu stream.

Vashisht Hot Springs and Temple — A small village near Manali known for its natural sulphur hot springs and an ancient temple dedicated to sage Vashisht, offering a relaxing soak alongside a dose of local mythology.

Manu Temple — Dedicated to the sage Manu, believed to be the site where he meditated after the great flood, this temple in Old Manali offers a quieter, more contemplative counterpoint to the town's adventure-heavy reputation.

Getting Around

Manali town itself is walkable, with Old Manali and the Mall Road easily covered on foot, but reaching Solang Valley, Rohtang Pass, or Vashisht requires taxis or hired vehicles given the distances and winding mountain roads. Permits are mandatory for Rohtang Pass and need to be arranged in advance, either online or through local agents in Manali.

What to Eat

Manali's food scene blends Himachali dishes like siddu and trout preparations with a strong presence of Tibetan, Israeli, and continental cafes, especially in Old Manali. Apple-based products, given the region's orchards, are also worth trying, from fresh apples to local ciders and jams.

Shopping

Mall Road and the markets around it are good for Himachali shawls, caps, and woollens, along with Tibetan handicrafts and local honey. Old Manali's shops lean more bohemian, offering handmade jewellery, clothing, and travel gear catering to the backpacker crowd.

What to Wear

Warm, layered clothing is essential year-round given the altitude, with heavy winter gear necessary between November and February when snowfall is common. Sturdy shoes matter for both town walking and any excursions to Solang Valley or Rohtang Pass, where terrain and snow conditions demand proper footwear.

The Feel of the Place

Manali carries an energetic, adventure-driven buzz that's distinct from the more sedate hill stations of Himachal — a mix of honeymooners, backpackers, and serious trekkers all drawn by the same dramatic mountain backdrop. Old Manali's café culture and Rohtang's high-altitude drama sit side by side, giving the town a genuinely varied character depending on which part you're in.

Festivals Worth Timing Your Trip Around

Manali Winter Carnival (January): A lively festival featuring skiing competitions, cultural performances, and local food, celebrating the town's winter tourism season.

Kullu Dussehra (October, nearby): One of India's most famous Dussehra celebrations, held in nearby Kullu with a week-long procession of local deities, drawing visitors from across the region for its unique scale and colour.

A Tip From Experience

Book Rohtang Pass permits well in advance, ideally online — this can't be overstated, since daily visitor numbers are capped and last-minute arrivals frequently find themselves unable to get a slot. Base yourself in Old Manali rather than the main town if you're after a quieter, more relaxed atmosphere, since it offers easy access to both nature and a livelier café scene without the congestion of Mall Road.

A Simple Full-Day Plan

Start the morning at Hadimba Devi Temple, followed by a walk through Old Manali and a stop at Manu Temple. Head to Vashisht for a relaxing dip in the hot springs by early afternoon, and if permits and time allow, dedicate a separate day entirely to Solang Valley or Rohtang Pass given the distances involved.

Best Time to Go

March to June offers pleasant weather and blooming orchards, ideal for sightseeing before the peak summer rush. October to February brings snow, particularly at Solang and Rohtang, making it the preferred season for winter sports enthusiasts, though Rohtang Pass itself closes for several months during peak winter.

How to Get There

The nearest airport is Bhuntar (Kullu-Manali Airport), roughly 50 km away, with limited but growing flight connectivity. The nearest major railway station is Joginder Nagar (narrow gauge) or, more practically, Chandigarh, about 310 km away, from where most travellers continue by road, given Manali's strong highway connectivity via NH3.
